The Normal Distribution

▶ Watch on YouTube Also in: —

This video explains the normal distribution, the bell-shaped curve. It shows what the curve means and which two numbers draw it.

In one breath

A normal distribution is a symmetrical bell-shaped curve. Its height shows how likely a measurement is. The curve always sits on the average value, and the standard deviation sets its width. A wide curve is short, a narrow curve tall. About 95% of measurements fall within two standard deviations of the mean.

The main idea

Josh starts with human height. People can be short, average or tall, or anywhere in between. The x-axis holds the measurement, so here it holds height.

The y-axis is the relative probability of seeing that measurement. Someone super short is rare, so the curve is low on the left. Someone near the average is common, so the curve is tall in the middle. Someone really tall is rare again, so the curve drops on the right. That is why the shape looks like a bell.

The curve is symmetrical: the left half mirrors the right half. It is also called the Gaussian distribution.

Step by step

  1. Put the measurement on the x-axis. Here it is human height in inches.
  2. Put the relative probability on the y-axis. A tall part means that measurement is common.
  3. Find the average. The centre of the bell sits on this value.
  4. Find the standard deviation. It sets how wide the bell is.
  5. Let the width decide the height. A wider curve is shorter, a narrower curve taller.
  6. Read the useful range: about 95% of measurements fall within two standard deviations of the mean.

Josh then puts two curves on one picture: newborn boys and adult men. They have different centres and very different widths.

Formulas

The video does not write the equation. It gives one practical rule, so that comes first. The second formula is background.

The 95% rule from the video
$$ \mu - 2\sigma \;\le\; x \;\le\; \mu + 2\sigma \quad\text{for about }95\%\text{ of measurements} $$

Symbols: \(\mu\) is the mean, the average of all measurements. \(\sigma\) is the standard deviation, the usual distance from the mean. \(x\) is one measurement. What it tells you: two numbers name the range that holds almost all the data.

The curve itself (background)
$$ f(x)=\frac{1}{\sigma\sqrt{2\pi}}\;e^{-\frac{(x-\mu)^2}{2\sigma^2}} $$

Symbols: \(f(x)\) is the height of the curve above measurement \(x\), the relative probability. \(\mu\) is the mean, \(\sigma\) the standard deviation, and \(e\) the constant 2.718. What it tells you: only \(\mu\) and \(\sigma\) appear, so two numbers draw the whole curve. The peak height is \(1/(\sigma\sqrt{2\pi})\), so a bigger \(\sigma\) gives a lower peak. Wider curves are shorter.

Worked example from the video

Example

Newborn boys have a mean height of 20 inches and a standard deviation of 0.6. Adult men have a mean of 70 inches and a standard deviation of 4.

For the babies, two standard deviations is \(2 \times 0.6 = 1.2\) inches, so 95% fall between 18.8 and 21.2 inches. For the adults, two standard deviations is \(2 \times 4 = 8\) inches, so 95% fall between 62 and 78 inches.

The baby range is 2.4 inches wide, the adult range 16 inches. Adult height has many more possible values, so each is less likely and the curve is lower. Josh sums it up: a newborn very likely falls within 1.2 inches of its mean, but a man rarely does.

Common mistakes and subtle points

Watch out The y-axis is a relative probability, not the probability of one exact value. Real probabilities come from area under the curve.
Watch out A taller curve does not mean more people. Both curves cover the same total probability. The baby curve is taller because it is narrower.
Watch out The two standard deviation rule comes from the bell shape. If your data is skewed or has two peaks, that range will not hold 95%.

If they ask you in an interview

What defines a normal distribution?
Two numbers define it completely. The mean sets the centre of the bell, and the standard deviation sets its width. Once the width is fixed the height follows, because a wider curve must be shorter.
What does the y-axis of a normal curve mean?
It is the relative probability of seeing that measurement. Where the curve is tall, that measurement is common. It is not the chance of one exact value; that comes from area.
Why is one normal curve taller than another?
Because it is narrower. The video compares newborn heights, standard deviation 0.6 inches, with adult heights, standard deviation 4 inches. Adult height has far more possible values, so each is less likely.
Given a mean and standard deviation, what range covers most data?
Mean plus or minus two standard deviations covers about 95%. For adult men that is 62 to 78 inches. I would check first that the data looks bell-shaped.

Recap in 30 seconds

  • The normal distribution is a symmetrical bell-shaped curve, also called Gaussian.
  • The x-axis holds the measurement and the y-axis the relative probability.
  • The curve is tall where measurements are common and low where they are rare.
  • The curve is always centred on the average of the measurements.
  • The standard deviation sets the width, and the width sets the height.
  • Wider curves are shorter, because the total probability stays the same.
  • About 95% of measurements fall within two standard deviations of the mean.
  • Height, weight and commuting times are close to normal, and the central limit theorem explains why.
