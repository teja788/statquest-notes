Regularization Part 3: Elastic Net Regression

▶ Watch on YouTube

Elastic Net Regression joins the Lasso penalty and the Ridge penalty in one model. Use it when you have many variables and cannot tell which ones are useful.

In one breath

Lasso works best when many variables are useless, because it can set their slopes to zero. Ridge works best when most variables are useful, because it only shrinks slopes. With millions of parameters you cannot know which case you are in. Elastic Net avoids the choice. It adds both penalties to least squares, each with its own lambda.

The main idea

Josh starts from the two earlier methods. Lasso Regression can remove terms, so it suits a model full of useless variables. Ridge Regression only shrinks terms, so it suits a model where most variables help. When you know your variables well, the choice is easy.

The problem appears with very large models. Josh mentions a deep learning conference where models had millions of parameters. Nobody can inspect that many. You almost certainly need regularization, but you cannot tell which penalty fits.

Elastic Net solves this by refusing to pick. It starts with least squares, then adds both penalties together. Each penalty gets its own lambda: \(\lambda_1\) for Lasso and \(\lambda_2\) for Ridge. Cross validation over combinations of the two lambdas finds the best pair.

Step by step

  1. Start with least squares, the fit that makes the squared residuals as small as possible.
  2. Add the Lasso penalty: \(\lambda_1\) times the sum of the absolute slopes.
  3. Add the Ridge penalty: \(\lambda_2\) times the sum of the squared slopes.
  4. Try many combinations of the two lambdas and keep the pair that predicts new data best.
  5. Read the corners. Both lambdas zero gives least squares. Only \(\lambda_1\) gives Lasso. Only \(\lambda_2\) gives Ridge.

The hybrid has one more strength. On its own, Lasso tends to pick just one of a group of correlated terms and delete the rest. Ridge shrinks all the correlated slopes together but never removes them. Elastic Net groups the correlated variables, then keeps them all or removes them all at once.

Formulas

Step 1 — least squares
$$ \text{SSR} \;=\; \sum_{i=1}^{n}\bigl(y_i-\hat{y}_i\bigr)^2 $$

Symbols: \(n\) is the number of data points, \(y_i\) the real measured value, \(\hat{y}_i\) the predicted value. SSR is the sum of the squared residuals. What it tells you: the plain fit quality that every method here starts from.

Step 2 — the two penalties
$$ \lambda_1\sum_{j=1}^{p}\lvert \beta_j \rvert \qquad\text{and}\qquad \lambda_2\sum_{j=1}^{p}\beta_j^{\,2} $$

Symbols: \(\beta_j\) is the slope for variable \(j\), and \(p\) is the number of variables. \(\lambda_1\) scales the Lasso penalty, \(\lambda_2\) the Ridge penalty. What it tells you: the absolute value lets a slope reach zero, so a variable can leave. Squaring only shrinks, so every variable stays.

Elastic Net — the two together
$$ \sum_{i=1}^{n}\bigl(y_i-\hat{y}_i\bigr)^2 \;+\; \lambda_1\sum_{j=1}^{p}\lvert \beta_j \rvert \;+\; \lambda_2\sum_{j=1}^{p}\beta_j^{\,2} $$

Symbols: the same symbols, with both lambdas present at once. What it tells you: Elastic Net minimises this one quantity, so it can shrink like Ridge and delete like Lasso in the same fit. Cross validation picks the best pair \((\lambda_1,\lambda_2)\).

Worked example from the video

Example

Josh predicts size from four variables: weight, high fat diet, astrological sign, and the air speed of a swallow. Weight and diet are useful. The other two are useless.

Elastic Net minimises this quantity:

$$ \text{SSR} \;+\; \lambda_1\bigl(\lvert \beta_{\text{wt}}\rvert+\lvert \beta_{\text{diet}}\rvert+\lvert \beta_{\text{sign}}\rvert+\lvert \beta_{\text{swallow}}\rvert\bigr) \;+\; \lambda_2\bigl(\beta_{\text{wt}}^{2}+\beta_{\text{diet}}^{2}+\beta_{\text{sign}}^{2}+\beta_{\text{swallow}}^{2}\bigr) $$

Both lambdas at zero makes the penalties vanish, so you get the least squares estimates. Only \(\lambda_1>0\) leaves the Lasso part, so the slopes for sign and swallow speed can reach zero. Only \(\lambda_2>0\) leaves the Ridge part, so all four slopes shrink and stay.

Common mistakes and subtle points

Watch out Elastic Net has two lambdas, not one. You must search over combinations of \(\lambda_1\) and \(\lambda_2\). A single one dimensional search is not enough.
Watch out Elastic Net is not a new kind of model. It is still least squares with penalties added.
Watch out Do not expect Lasso to handle correlated variables well. It keeps one of them and drops the others, so the survivor looks uniquely important when it is not.

If they ask you in an interview

What is Elastic Net Regression?
It is least squares with two penalties added at once. One is the Lasso penalty, the sum of the absolute slopes, scaled by lambda one. The other is the Ridge penalty, the sum of the squared slopes, scaled by lambda two. Cross validation tunes both.
When would you choose Elastic Net over Lasso or Ridge?
When the model has far too many variables to judge one by one. Lasso suits mostly useless variables, Ridge mostly useful ones. If you cannot tell which case you face, Elastic Net lets the data decide.
Why is Elastic Net better with correlated variables?
Lasso picks one variable from a correlated group and removes the rest. Ridge shrinks the whole group together but never removes anything. Elastic Net combines both, so it groups the correlated variables and keeps them all or removes them all together.
What happens at the extreme lambda values?
Both lambdas at zero gives plain least squares. Lambda one positive with lambda two at zero is exactly Lasso. The reverse is exactly Ridge. Both positive gives the hybrid people mean by Elastic Net.

Recap in 30 seconds

  • Lasso suits models with many useless variables, because it can set slopes to zero.
  • Ridge suits models where most variables are useful, because it only shrinks slopes.
  • With huge models you cannot know in advance which case applies to you.
  • Elastic Net starts with least squares and adds both penalties at once.
  • Cross validation over combinations of the two lambdas finds the best pair.
  • Setting one lambda to zero returns you to plain Lasso or plain Ridge.
  • Elastic Net also groups correlated variables and keeps or removes them together.
