Encoder-Only Transformers (like BERT) for RAG

▶ Watch on YouTube Also in: Neural Networks / Deep Learning (video 22)

Encoder-only Transformers, like BERT, turn text into context-aware embeddings. These embeddings let us cluster similar sentences and documents, classify text, and find relevant text for retrieval augmented generation (RAG). In RAG, an AI first retrieves the text chunks most similar to a prompt.

In one breath

The first Transformer, from 2017, had an encoder and a decoder that worked together to translate text. People soon found that each half works on its own. Decoders became the basis for ChatGPT, and encoders the basis for BERT. An encoder-only Transformer is built from three blocks: word embedding, positional encoding, and self-attention. Together they give each token a context-aware embedding that reflects meaning, word order, and relationships among words. These embeddings can cluster similar sentences or documents, feed a classifier, and power the retrieval step in RAG.

The main idea

Josh starts with history. The first Transformer had an encoder and a decoder that were connected. It was an encoder-decoder, or sequence-to-sequence (seq2seq), model built to translate text. If Squatch says "pizza is great", the encoder crunches numbers to encode the input. The decoder then uses those numbers to produce the Spanish translation.

People then realised the two parts work fine alone. Decoder-only Transformers generate text, and they form the basis for ChatGPT and Gemini. Encoder-only Transformers form the basis for BERT and many other models. Decoder-only models got most of the attention, so Josh wants to show the understated power of encoder-only models.

The key difference is what each model is used for. A decoder-only model produces text. An encoder-only model produces context-aware embeddings. These are numbers for each token that account for its position and its links to other words. Those numbers are what we use for clustering, classification, and RAG.

Step by step

  1. Word embedding turns tokens into numbers. Tokens are words, bits of words, and symbols. Transformers are neural networks, and neural networks only work with numbers.
  2. Random numbers are a poor choice. If each word gets a random number, "great" and "awesome" get very different numbers. Learning to use "great" then does not help with "awesome", so training needs more complexity.
  3. We want similar words to get similar numbers, and more than one number per word. "Great" can be positive ("pizza is great") or sarcastic ("my cell phone's broken, great"). Several numbers per word let the network keep track of different uses.
  4. A simple neural network learns the embeddings. It has one input and one output per unique word. The number of activation functions sets how many numbers represent each word. The weights start random and are trained so each word predicts the next word.
  5. More context helps, but order is lost. We can use the preceding four words, "the pizza came out", to predict "of". However, this ignores order, so a jumble like "pizza out came the" gives the same inputs.
  6. Positional encoding keeps track of word order. "Squatch eats pizza" and "pizza eats Squatch" use the same words but mean very different things. There are many ways to do positional encoding; the details are outside this video.
  7. Self-attention keeps track of relationships among words. In "the pizza came out of the oven and it tasted good", the word "it" should link to "pizza", not "oven". Self-attention measures how similar each word is to every word in the sentence, including itself. Those similarities decide how each word is encoded. Encoder-only Transformers use only self-attention.
  8. The result is a context-aware embedding. Combining the three blocks gives each token a new embedding, also called a contextualized embedding. Because it includes position and relationships, it can help cluster similar sentences or even similar documents.
  9. RAG uses these embeddings for retrieval. RAG breaks a document into blocks of text and creates context-aware embeddings for each block. When someone asks an AI "What is pizza?", RAG embeds the prompt and finds the most similar chunks of text.
  10. The embeddings also work as features for classification. They can be inputs to a normal neural network that classifies sentiment, for example positive or negative posts about pizza on LinkedIn. They can also be variables in a logistic regression model.

Formulas

The video gives no equations. It focuses on the main ideas and leaves the detailed math of positional encoding and attention to other videos. The two lines below only summarise the order of operations the video describes. They are a memory aid, not the real math.

Context-aware embedding (schematic)
$$ \mathbf{c}_i = \text{SelfAttention}\Big(\text{PositionalEncoding}\big(\text{WordEmbedding}(w_i)\big)\Big) $$

Symbols: \(w_i\) is the token at position \(i\). WordEmbedding turns the token into numbers. PositionalEncoding adds information about where the token sits. SelfAttention compares the token with every token in the sentence, including itself. \(\mathbf{c}_i\) is the resulting context-aware embedding. What it tells you: the same word can get different embeddings in different sentences, because order and neighbours change the result.

RAG retrieval (schematic)
$$ \text{best chunk} = \arg\max_{k}\ \text{similarity}\big(\mathbf{e}_{\text{prompt}},\ \mathbf{e}_k\big) $$

Symbols: \(\mathbf{e}_{\text{prompt}}\) is the embedding of the prompt, such as "What is pizza?". \(\mathbf{e}_k\) is the embedding of text block \(k\). "similarity" is how alike two embeddings are; the video does not name a specific measure. \(\arg\max_k\) picks the block with the highest similarity. What it tells you: retrieval means embedding everything with the same encoder and picking the closest chunks.

Common mistakes and subtle points

Watch out Plain word embeddings are not context-aware embeddings. Word embeddings place similar words near each other. Context-aware embeddings also include word order and relationships, which is what lets them cluster whole sentences and documents.
Watch out Using more words as context does not by itself capture order. Without positional encoding, any jumble of the same words looks the same to the model.
Watch out There are different types of attention. Encoder-only Transformers use only self-attention, where each word is compared with every word in the sentence, including itself.
Watch out Do not assume encoder-only models are obsolete because decoder-only models get the hype. Their embeddings drive clustering, classification, and the retrieval step of RAG.

If they ask you in an interview

How does an encoder-only Transformer differ from a decoder-only one?
Both come from the original 2017 encoder-decoder Transformer built for translation. Decoder-only models, like the ones behind ChatGPT, generate text. Encoder-only models, like BERT, use word embedding, positional encoding, and self-attention to create context-aware embeddings. We use those embeddings for clustering, classification, and retrieval.
What is a context-aware embedding?
It is the embedding a token gets after word embedding, positional encoding, and self-attention are combined. It reflects the word's meaning, its position, and its relationships with the other words. So "it" in a sentence about pizza coming out of an oven gets linked to pizza, not oven.
How does RAG use an encoder-only Transformer?
RAG breaks documents into blocks of text and creates a context-aware embedding for each block. When a prompt arrives, such as "What is pizza?", it embeds the prompt the same way. It then finds the chunks whose embeddings are most similar to the prompt's embedding.
Why do we need positional encoding and self-attention on top of word embeddings?
Word embeddings alone ignore order, so "Squatch eats pizza" and "pizza eats Squatch" look the same. Positional encoding fixes that. Self-attention then captures relationships among words by measuring how similar each word is to every other word.
How would you use these embeddings for classification?
I would feed the context-aware embeddings into a normal neural network that predicts a label, such as positive or negative sentiment about pizza posts. Alternatively, I could use them as variables in a logistic regression model.

Recap in 30 seconds

  • The first Transformer had an encoder and a decoder, and each part later proved useful on its own.
  • Decoder-only models like ChatGPT generate text, while encoder-only models like BERT create embeddings.
  • Word embedding turns tokens into numbers, and training on next-word prediction puts similar words like great and awesome close together.
  • Positional encoding keeps track of word order, which plain word embeddings ignore.
  • Self-attention compares each word with every word, so "it" can be linked to "pizza".
  • Together these blocks give context-aware embeddings that can cluster similar sentences and documents.
  • RAG embeds text blocks and the prompt, then retrieves the most similar blocks.
  • The same embeddings can feed a neural network or logistic regression for classification.
