Neural Networks Part 7: Cross Entropy Derivatives and Backpropagation

▶ Watch on YouTube Also in: Neural Networks / Deep Learning (video 13)

This video shows how to take the derivative of cross entropy with respect to one bias in a neural network with a softmax output. It then uses those derivatives in backpropagation to optimize that bias.

In one breath

A neural network predicts iris species, and softmax turns its raw outputs into probabilities. Cross entropy measures how good each prediction is. Josh uses the chain rule to link cross entropy to the bias \(b_3\), which only feeds the raw output for Setosa. When the observed species is Setosa, the derivative is \(p_{\text{setosa}} - 1\). For any other species it is just \(p_{\text{setosa}}\). Gradient descent uses these derivatives to move \(b_3\) from -2 to its best value, -0.03.

The main idea

The network takes petal width and sepal width as inputs. Two hidden nodes each make a bent surface: the node's output, plotted over every pair of inputs. Josh colours them blue and orange. For Setosa, the blue surface is scaled by -0.1 and the orange surface by 1.5. Adding them, plus a final bias \(b_3\), gives a green crinkled surface. This is the raw output for Setosa, the number that comes out before softmax.

Versicolor and Virginica get their own raw outputs in the same way, each with its own bias. Softmax then turns the three raw outputs into three predicted probabilities. Cross entropy takes the predicted probability for the observed species and gives an error score.

To optimize \(b_3\) with gradient descent, we need the derivative of cross entropy with respect to \(b_3\). Cross entropy is linked to \(b_3\) through a predicted probability and the raw output for Setosa. So the chain rule gives the answer as a product of three smaller derivatives.

Step by step

  1. Put petal and sepal widths into the network. The blue and orange bent surfaces are scaled and added, then \(b_3\) is added to give the raw output for Setosa.
  2. Run the three raw outputs through softmax. For the Setosa training row in the video, the predicted probability for Setosa is 0.57, and cross entropy is 0.56.
  3. Notice that \(b_3\) changes the Setosa raw output. That raw output is in the numerator and denominator of the Setosa softmax, and in the denominators for Versicolor and Virginica.
  4. Cross entropy uses the probability for the observed species. So a Setosa row and a Virginica row give different cross entropy equations, and different derivatives.
  5. Derive the Setosa case with the chain rule. The answer simplifies to \(p_{\text{setosa}} - 1\).
  6. Derive the Virginica case the same way. The answer simplifies to \(p_{\text{setosa}}\). Versicolor gives the same result, so Josh leaves it as homework.
  7. Start \(b_3\) at -2. Add the derivatives from all training rows and take a gradient descent step. Repeat until the predictions stop improving.

Formulas

Raw output and softmax
$$ r_{s} = (\text{blue}\times -0.1) + (\text{orange}\times 1.5) + b_3 \qquad p_{s} = \frac{e^{r_{s}}}{e^{r_{s}} + e^{r_{ve}} + e^{r_{vi}}} $$

Symbols: \(r_s, r_{ve}, r_{vi}\) are the raw outputs for Setosa, Versicolor and Virginica. "blue" and "orange" are the two bent surfaces from the hidden nodes. \(b_3\) is the final bias for Setosa. \(p_s\) is the predicted probability for Setosa. What it tells you: \(b_3\) enters only \(r_s\), but \(r_s\) appears in every softmax output.

Chain rule, observed species is Setosa
$$ \frac{d\,CE}{d\,b_3} = \frac{d\,CE}{d\,p_s}\cdot\frac{d\,p_s}{d\,r_s}\cdot\frac{d\,r_s}{d\,b_3}, \qquad CE = -\ln(p_s) $$

Symbols: \(CE\) is the cross entropy for one row. \(p_s\) is the softmax output for Setosa. \(r_s\) is the raw output for Setosa. What it tells you: we split one hard derivative into three easy ones along the path from cross entropy back to \(b_3\).

$$ \frac{d\,CE}{d\,p_s} = -\frac{1}{p_s} $$

Cross entropy to softmax: the derivative of \(-\ln(p_s)\) is \(-1\) divided by \(p_s\).

$$ \frac{d\,p_s}{d\,r_s} = p_s(1 - p_s) $$

Softmax to raw output: this is the softmax derivative from an earlier video, which Josh asks us to take on trust here.

$$ \frac{d\,r_s}{d\,b_3} = 0 + 0 + 1 = 1 $$

Raw output to bias: the blue and orange surfaces do not depend on \(b_3\), so only the \(b_3\) term leaves a 1.

$$ \frac{d\,CE}{d\,b_3} = -\frac{1}{p_s}\cdot p_s(1-p_s)\cdot 1 = p_s - 1 $$

Multiply, cancel the two \(p_s\) terms, and move the negative sign in to get the familiar result.

Chain rule, observed species is Virginica
$$ \frac{d\,CE}{d\,b_3} = \frac{d\,CE}{d\,p_{vi}}\cdot\frac{d\,p_{vi}}{d\,r_s}\cdot\frac{d\,r_s}{d\,b_3}, \qquad CE = -\ln(p_{vi}) $$

Symbols: \(p_{vi}\) is the softmax output for Virginica. The path still goes through \(r_s\), because that is the only raw output \(b_3\) touches. What it tells you: the same three-link chain works, but the middle link changes.

$$ \frac{d\,CE}{d\,p_{vi}} = -\frac{1}{p_{vi}} $$

Cross entropy to softmax: same rule as before, now for the Virginica probability.

$$ \frac{d\,p_{vi}}{d\,r_s} = -\,p_s\, p_{vi} $$

Softmax to raw output: a change in the Setosa raw output moves the Virginica probability through its denominator only.

$$ \frac{d\,r_s}{d\,b_3} = 1 $$

Raw output to bias: this link is exactly the same as in the Setosa case.

$$ \frac{d\,CE}{d\,b_3} = -\frac{1}{p_{vi}}\cdot(-p_s\,p_{vi})\cdot 1 = p_s $$

The two \(p_{vi}\) terms cancel and the two negatives cancel, leaving the Setosa probability. Versicolor gives the same answer.

Gradient descent update
$$ \text{step size} = \text{slope}\times\text{learning rate}, \qquad b_3^{\text{new}} = b_3^{\text{old}} - \text{step size} $$

Symbols: the slope is the sum of the per-row derivatives of cross entropy with respect to \(b_3\). The learning rate scales the step. What it tells you: a negative slope pushes \(b_3\) up, toward lower total cross entropy.

Worked example from the video

Example

There are three training rows: Setosa, Virginica and Versicolor. Josh sets \(b_3 = -2\). At that value the total cross entropy is 2.67. If you try many values of \(b_3\), the total cross entropy traces a curve, and we want its lowest point.

For each row, pick the derivative that matches the observed species. Then run the row through the network to get \(p_s\).

Row 1 is Setosa with \(p_s = 0.15\), so its term is \(0.15 - 1 = -0.85\). Row 2 is Virginica with \(p_s = 0.04\), so its term is \(0.04\). Row 3 is Versicolor with \(p_s = 0.04\), so its term is \(0.04\).

$$ \text{slope} = -0.85 + 0.04 + 0.04 = -0.77 $$

With a learning rate of 1, the step size is -0.77. The new bias is:

$$ b_3^{\text{new}} = -2 - (-0.77) = -1.23 $$

Josh repeats this with each new value of \(b_3\). The predictions stop improving when \(b_3 = -0.03\).

Common mistakes and subtle points

Watch out The derivative for a row depends on which species was observed in that row. A Setosa row uses \(p_s - 1\), while Virginica and Versicolor rows use \(p_s\). Mixing them up gives the wrong slope.
Watch out The \(p_s\) in each term comes from that row's own measurements. The Setosa probability for the Virginica row is not the same number as for the Setosa row. Josh says this difference matters when you do backpropagation.
Watch out These results are for \(b_3\) only, because \(b_3\) only directly affects the raw output for Setosa. Derivatives with respect to \(b_4\) (Versicolor) or \(b_5\) (Virginica) follow the same process but give different results.
Watch out The derivative of the raw output with respect to \(b_3\) is 1 because the blue and orange surfaces are made before \(b_3\) is added. Their derivatives with respect to \(b_3\) are 0.

If they ask you in an interview

What is the gradient of softmax plus cross entropy with respect to the output bias?
For the bias on the Setosa raw output, it is \(p_s - 1\) when the observed class is Setosa. It is \(p_s\) when the observed class is something else. You get this by chaining three pieces: minus one over p, the softmax derivative, and one for the bias.
Why does a bias on one class output affect the loss for rows of another class?
The bias changes that class's raw output. That raw output sits in the denominator of every softmax probability. So even when cross entropy uses the Virginica probability, it still depends on the Setosa bias.
How do you get the gradient over the whole training set?
You compute the derivative for each row, using the formula that matches the observed class. Then you add them up. In the video the three terms are -0.85, 0.04 and 0.04, which sum to a slope of -0.77.
What does gradient descent do with that slope?
It multiplies the slope by the learning rate to get a step size, then subtracts the step size from the old value. With a learning rate of 1, the bias moves from -2 to -1.23. You repeat until predictions stop improving or you hit a step limit.

Recap in 30 seconds

  • Softmax turns raw outputs into probabilities, and cross entropy scores the probability for the observed species.
  • The bias \(b_3\) only directly affects the raw output for Setosa.
  • The chain rule links cross entropy to \(b_3\) through a softmax probability and the Setosa raw output.
  • When the observed species is Setosa, the derivative is \(p_s - 1\).
  • When the observed species is Virginica or Versicolor, the derivative is \(p_s\).
  • Add the per-row derivatives to get the slope, which was -0.77 at \(b_3 = -2\).
  • Gradient descent moved \(b_3\) to -1.23, and repeated steps stopped at -0.03.
