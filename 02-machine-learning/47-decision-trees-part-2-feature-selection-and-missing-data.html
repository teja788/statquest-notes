Decision Trees, Part 2 - Feature Selection and Missing Data

▶ Watch on YouTube

A decision tree can drop a useless column by itself. It can also work around holes in your table.

In one breath

A tree splits on a column only when the split lowers impurity, which measures how mixed the groups are. A column that never lowers impurity never enters the tree, and that is automatic feature selection. Demanding a large drop gives simpler trees that are less overfit. A missing value can be filled with the most common answer, the mean or median, or a guess from a matching column.

The main idea

In the first decision tree video, Josh built a tree for heart disease. It asked about good blood circulation, then blocked arteries, then chest pain. Down one path, 17 people with those answers had heart disease and 3 did not.

Down another path the tree stopped early. For patients with good circulation and no blocked arteries, Josh compared the impurity after splitting on chest pain with the impurity of no split. No split was lower, so that node became a leaf.

Now imagine chest pain never lowers impurity anywhere. Then it never enters the tree, even though the column sits in your data. That is automatic feature selection. You can also demand a large drop before any split. Weak splits are refused, so the tree stays small and is less overfit. Overfit means good on the training data, but bad on new data.

Step by step

  1. At a node, compute the impurity after splitting on a column such as chest pain.
  2. Compute the impurity with no split, as a leaf.
  3. Keep the split only if impurity drops enough to pass your threshold. A column that never wins never enters the tree.
  4. For a missing category, take the most common answer, or copy it from the closest column.
  5. For a missing number, take the mean or median, or a least squares prediction.

Formulas

Impurity reduction, with a threshold
$$ \Delta I = I(\text{node}) - \left( \frac{n_L}{n}\,I(\text{left}) + \frac{n_R}{n}\,I(\text{right}) \right), \qquad \text{split only if } \Delta I > \tau $$

Symbols: \(I(\text{node})\) is the impurity with no split. \(I(\text{left})\) and \(I(\text{right})\) are the impurities of the two groups after the split, with counts \(n_L\) and \(n_R\) out of \(n\). \(\Delta I\) is the drop in impurity, and \(\tau\) is the threshold you choose. What it tells you: a column enters the tree only when it beats \(\tau\), so a larger \(\tau\) gives a simpler tree.

Filling a missing number from a correlated column
$$ \widehat{\text{weight}} = b_0 + b_1 \times \text{height} $$

Symbols: \(\widehat{\text{weight}}\) is the predicted weight for the patient with the gap, and \(\text{height}\) is that patient's known height. \(b_0\) is the intercept and \(b_1\) the slope, both from the least squares fit on patients who have both values. What it tells you: the column you have fills in for the column you lack.

Worked example from the video

Example

In the first video, one patient had no answer for blocked arteries, so Josh skipped that patient. Here he fills the gap. The simple fix is the most common answer in that column.

Another fix uses chest pain, the column that matches blocked arteries most closely. Patients 1 and 3 have no in both columns, and patient 2 has yes in both. Patient 4 has yes for chest pain, so blocked arteries becomes yes.

For a number such as weight, use the mean or the median. Or fit a least squares line on height, which tracks weight closely.

Common mistakes and subtle points

Watch out Having data for a column does not mean the tree uses it. Chest pain was in the table, but it left the tree because it never lowered impurity.
Watch out The threshold is a trade. A large threshold gives a simple tree, but it can refuse a split that would have helped.
Watch out Every fill is a guess. The mean and the median ignore the rest of the patient's row. A matching column, such as chest pain or height, uses more of the data.

If they ask you in an interview

How does a decision tree do feature selection?
At every node the tree compares the impurity after a split with the impurity of no split, and keeps the split only when impurity drops. A column that never wins that comparison never appears in the tree. The selection happens while the tree is built.
How would you stop a decision tree from overfitting?
Trees often overfit. One fix is to require a minimum drop in impurity before you allow a split. Weak splits are refused, so the tree stays small and leans less on quirks of the training data.
What are your options when a column has missing values?
For a category, use the most common value, or copy the answer from the column that matches it best. For a number, use the mean or median, or predict it from a least squares line against the most correlated column. Correlation means two columns tend to move together, so one column can guide the guess for the other.
