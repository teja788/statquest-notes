Reinforcement Learning with Neural Networks: Mathematical Details

▶ Watch on YouTube Also in: —

This video shows every mathematical step needed to train one bias in a neural network with policy gradients. The key idea is simple. Compute a derivative based on a guess. Then multiply it by a reward that tells you if the guess was right.

In one breath

A small network takes hunger as input and outputs the probability of going to Norm's Fry Hut instead of Squatch's Fry Shack. We pick a place at random using those probabilities and guess that this choice was correct. Cross entropy measures the gap, and the chain rule gives its derivative with respect to the bias. Then we order fries. The reward is +1 for a correct guess or −1 for a wrong one, and it multiplies the derivative. Gradient descent uses this updated derivative to move the bias, and the probabilities slowly shift toward the better choice.

The main idea

In normal backpropagation we know the correct answer before training. Here we do not. We cannot know in advance if Squatch or Norm will give us a large or a small order of fries. So we cannot train the network with standard backpropagation.

Policy gradients solve this with a guess. Whatever place we randomly chose, we pretend it was the right choice. That means the ideal probability for that place is 1. Cross entropy measures how far the network's probability is from that ideal. We take the derivative of the cross entropy with respect to the bias, exactly as in backpropagation.

The derivative points the right way only if the guess was correct. So after we get our fries, we multiply the derivative by a reward. A reward of +1 keeps the direction. A reward of −1 flips it and corrects the mistake. Only then do we take the gradient descent step.

Step by step

  1. Run the input through the network. With hunger 0.0, the output is PNorm = 0.5, so PSquatch = 1 − 0.5 = 0.5.
  2. Lay the two probabilities end to end on a line from 0 to 1. Pick a random number between 0 and 1. It lands on 0.2, which is in Squatch's region, so we go to Squatch.
  3. Guess that going to Squatch was correct. Then the ideal PSquatch is 1.0. Use cross entropy to measure the gap between 1.0 and 0.5.
  4. Use the chain rule to get the derivative of the cross entropy with respect to the bias. The chain rule multiplies the slopes of linked steps. Here there are three links: cross entropy to PNorm, PNorm to x, and x to the bias.
  5. Plug in PNorm = 0.5 to get a derivative of 0.5. On its own, gradient descent would shift the bias to the left.
  6. Order the fries. We get a small serving while not hungry, so the guess was correct and the reward is 1.0. Multiply the derivative by the reward.
  7. Compute the step size (learning rate × updated derivative) and subtract it from the old bias.
  8. Repeat with new random choices. Over many updates, using hunger values from 0 to 1, the bias starts to hover around −10. Then training is done.

Formulas

The network
$$ x = \text{hunger}\cdot w + b, \qquad P_{Norm} = \sigma(x) = \frac{1}{1+e^{-x}}, \qquad P_{Squatch} = 1 - P_{Norm} $$

Symbols: \(\text{hunger}\) is the input (0 means not hungry); \(w\) is the weight; \(b\) is the bias we train; \(x\) is the x-axis input to the sigmoid; \(\sigma\) is the sigmoid activation function, which squashes any number to between 0 and 1; \(P_{Norm}\) and \(P_{Squatch}\) are the probabilities of going to each place. What it tells you: the bias shifts \(x\), which changes both probabilities.

Cross entropy for the place we visited
$$ CE_{Squatch} = -\log(P_{Squatch}) = -\log(1 - P_{Norm}), \qquad CE_{Norm} = -\log(P_{Norm}) $$

Symbols: \(CE\) is cross entropy; \(\log\) is the natural log (base \(e\)). What it tells you: we use the cross entropy of whichever place we went to, because we guessed that place was correct.

Chain rule
$$ \frac{dCE}{db} = \frac{dCE}{dP_{Norm}} \cdot \frac{dP_{Norm}}{dx} \cdot \frac{dx}{db} $$

Symbols: each fraction is a derivative, the slope of one link in the chain. What it tells you: the cross entropy reaches the bias through \(P_{Norm}\) and \(x\), so we multiply the three local slopes.

First link: cross entropy to PNorm
$$ \frac{d}{dP_{Norm}}\big[-\log(1-P_{Norm})\big] = \frac{-1}{1-P_{Norm}}\cdot(-1) = \frac{1}{1-P_{Norm}}, \qquad \frac{d}{dP_{Norm}}\big[-\log P_{Norm}\big] = \frac{-1}{P_{Norm}} $$

Symbols: the left result is for visiting Squatch, the right one for visiting Norm. What it tells you: Josh lets "stuff" be \(1-P_{Norm}\); the derivative of \(-\log(\text{stuff})\) is \(-1/\text{stuff}\), and the derivative of stuff is \(-1\). This link is the only part that changes between the two places.

Second link: derivative of the sigmoid
$$ \frac{d}{dx}(1+e^{-x})^{-1} = -1\cdot(1+e^{-x})^{-2}\cdot(-e^{-x}) = \frac{e^{-x}}{(1+e^{-x})^2} $$ $$ = \frac{1}{1+e^{-x}}\cdot\frac{1+e^{-x}-1}{1+e^{-x}} = \sigma(x)\big(1-\sigma(x)\big) $$

Symbols: \(e\) is Euler's number; \(\sigma(x)\) is the sigmoid output. What it tells you: the power rule and chain rule give the first line. Splitting the square and adding +1 and −1 in the numerator gives the familiar form. Since \(\sigma(x) = P_{Norm}\), this link equals \(P_{Norm}(1-P_{Norm})\).

Third link and the full derivatives
$$ \frac{dx}{db} = 1, \qquad \frac{dCE_{Squatch}}{db} = \frac{1}{1-P_{Norm}}\cdot P_{Norm}(1-P_{Norm})\cdot 1, \qquad \frac{dCE_{Norm}}{db} = \frac{-1}{P_{Norm}}\cdot P_{Norm}(1-P_{Norm})\cdot 1 $$

Symbols: as above. What it tells you: the two derivatives differ only in the first factor. They simplify to \(P_{Norm}\) and \(-(1-P_{Norm})\).

Reward and gradient descent update
$$ \text{updated derivative} = \text{reward}\times\frac{dCE}{db}, \quad \text{step size} = \text{learning rate}\times\text{updated derivative}, \quad b_{new} = b_{old} - \text{step size} $$

Symbols: the reward is +1 for a correct guess and −1 for a wrong one; the learning rate is 1.0 in the video. What it tells you: the reward decides whether the bias moves in the direction the derivative suggests or the opposite way.

Worked example from the video

Example

Round 1. Hunger is 0.0 and the bias starts at 0.0, so \(x = 0\) and \(P_{Norm} = \sigma(0) = 0.5\). The random number 0.2 sends us to Squatch. The derivative is \(\frac{1}{1-0.5}\cdot 0.5\cdot(1-0.5)\cdot 1 = 2 \times 0.25 = 0.5\). Squatch gives a small serving and we are not hungry, so the guess was correct and the reward is 1.0. The updated derivative is \(1.0 \times 0.5 = 0.5\). The step size is \(1.0 \times 0.5 = 0.5\). The new bias is \(0.0 - 0.5 = -0.5\).

Round 2. With the new bias, \(P_{Norm}\) is 0.4 and \(P_{Squatch}\) rises to 0.6. The random number 0.9 sends us to Norm. The derivative is \(\frac{-1}{0.4}\cdot 0.4\cdot 0.6\cdot 1 = -0.6\). Norm gives a huge pile of fries while we are not hungry, so the guess was wrong and the reward is −1. The updated derivative is \(-1 \times -0.6 = 0.6\). The step size is \(1.0 \times 0.6 = 0.6\). The new bias is \(-0.5 - 0.6 = -1.1\).

Result. With bias −1.1 and hunger 0.0, \(P_{Norm}\) drops to about 0.2. Both visits at hunger 0.0 showed we should go to Squatch, so this is the right trend. The video uses rounded probabilities. The exact values are \(\sigma(-0.5) \approx 0.38\) and \(\sigma(-1.1) \approx 0.25\).

Common mistakes and subtle points

Watch out The derivative is based on a guess. If you plug it straight into gradient descent, a wrong guess pushes the bias the wrong way. Always multiply by the reward first.
Watch out The cross entropy depends on where you went. Visiting Squatch uses \(-\log(1-P_{Norm})\); visiting Norm uses \(-\log P_{Norm}\). Only the first link of the chain changes; the sigmoid and bias links stay the same.
Watch out Training on hunger 0.0 alone is not enough. The video says you need inputs from 0 to 1 and many updates before the bias settles near −10.
Watch out The rewards of +1 and −1 are a simple choice. Real systems often use more elaborate rewards, but the purpose is the same: update, and possibly correct, the derivative computed from the guess.

If they ask you in an interview

Why can't we train this network with standard backpropagation?
Backpropagation needs the correct answer for each input before training. Here we do not know in advance whether a place will give us a large or small order. So we guess that our random choice was right, compute the derivative from that guess, and fix its direction with a reward afterward.
Where exactly does the reward enter the policy gradient update?
We compute the derivative of the cross entropy with respect to the parameter as usual. Then we multiply that derivative by the reward before computing the step size. A positive reward keeps the direction, and a negative reward flips it.
What is the derivative of the sigmoid, and how do you get it?
It is \(\sigma(x)(1-\sigma(x))\). Write the sigmoid as \((1+e^{-x})^{-1}\) and apply the chain rule to get \(e^{-x}/(1+e^{-x})^2\). Splitting that into two fractions and adding and subtracting 1 in the numerator gives the familiar form.
Walk me through one update with numbers.
With hunger 0 and bias 0, the probability of Norm is 0.5, and we randomly go to Squatch. The derivative of the Squatch cross entropy with respect to the bias is 0.5. The guess was correct, so the reward is 1 and the updated derivative stays 0.5. With learning rate 1, the bias moves from 0 to −0.5.

Recap in 30 seconds

  • The network turns hunger into PNorm with a sigmoid, and PSquatch is 1 − PNorm.
  • We pick a place at random using these probabilities and guess that the choice was correct.
  • Cross entropy for the visited place measures the gap between its probability and the ideal of 1.
  • The chain rule gives the derivative with respect to the bias as three multiplied links, including the sigmoid derivative \(\sigma(x)(1-\sigma(x))\).
  • The reward, +1 for a correct guess and −1 for a wrong one, multiplies the derivative before the gradient descent step.
  • In the video, the bias moves from 0.0 to −0.5 to −1.1, and PNorm at hunger 0.0 falls from 0.5 to about 0.2.
  • With many updates over hunger values from 0 to 1, the bias settles near −10.
